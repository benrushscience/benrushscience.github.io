Origin story

A winding path into science, comedy, and communication.

Ben’s story moves through music, biology, public health, nutrition, AmeriCorps, podcasting, New Zealand, radiology, and improv.

The common thread is curiosity: following strange turns, learning through projects, and finding ways to make complicated ideas more human.

Curiosity Public health Science communication Madison

Ben grew up in Cincinnati, Ohio, experiencing a mix of the outdoors, frequent zoo visits, and a wealth of comedy. He was just as likely to be found building LEGO amusement parks as quoting the Holy Grail with his close friends. During his high school years, he composed music in FL Studio, consumed too much Adult Swim, and made abstract jokes with friends. (He even won a limo ride in 8th grade and dressed up for it, becoming an accidental pubescent Willy Wonka.) Ben originally applied to college for music composition and was rejected everywhere!

Ben then applied as a psychology major to Indiana University–Bloomington. His goal was to obtain a “Job.” He had some interest in science, but didn’t realize it could be a career until Eric Knox chatted with him about graduate school. Soon after, Ben switched to biology and neuroscience, took Integrated Physiology with Whitney Schlegel and became a teaching assistant for the class.

Ben didn’t know what to do next, so he started a Master of Public Health in epidemiology, also at IU–Bloomington, to get into more debt. This started his love for applied statistics and coding, specifically utilizing SAS, SPSS, and HLM.

After his master’s degree, Ben still didn’t know what to do, but he did see friends and family grow by becoming AmeriCorps members. Ben completed one AmeriCorps year as a Wilderness Therapy Counselor at Pfeifer Camp in Little Rock, Arkansas, and another as a Nutrition Educator at the Winnebago County Health Department in Oshkosh, Wisconsin. During these years, Ben visited Madison, Wisconsin, a few times and knew he had to live there. At the last second, he applied to become a personal chef with Chefs for Seniors. Eventually, he knew he should return to graduate school. He originally planned to study population health but, with a convenient click of a checkbox, also applied for nutrition.

Ben decided to pursue Nutritional Sciences at the University of Wisconsin-Madison (a good thing, because he was rejected from population health there, his first choice). He quickly became involved with the community on campus by working with Catalysts for Science Policy and off campus by becoming a council member of the Madison Food Policy Council. In January 2020, Ben took “Improv for Scientists” with Amy Zelenski, which taught him how to roll with chaos. COVID put that to the test a few months later. Living by himself, Ben wrote comedy and created characters to cope. Using his humor, science knowledge, and high school audio skills, he launched the “Deeper Than Data” podcast in February 2021. He kept up the podcast and accidental business past his PhD graduation in May 2022.

Ben was fortunate to have a cool boss and mentor, Adam Kuchnia. After earning his PhD, Ben worked as a scientist in Adam’s lab for a few months, including two months working remotely in New Zealand. A bit of work and mostly holiday made Ben a happy boy. He hiked mountains, met many lovely Kiwis (mostly in human form), saw glowworms, volcanoes, dolphins, and even kea in Milford Sound! The trip was legendary. Ben returned to the States for a bit, crashed on friends’ couches, traveled to Las Vegas for the 30th anniversary of Magic the Gathering, and then headed to France and Spain (fewer Kiwis). All this time, Ben reflected on how much he loved the intersection of humor and science.

At the crack of dawn, sitting in the passenger seat of a camper van looking at Abel Tasman National Park in NZ, Ben interviewed with Claudia Solis-Lemus about a postdoctoral researcher position. The two agreed to work together starting in January 2023. Ben worked with Claudia and split some time working with John Garrett at UW-Madison. Ben’s work focused on creating an educational website in data science and statistics, analyzing AI-derived CT tissue measurements, and developing AI applications for scientists. Ben brought his humor skills to science by creating the “Science and Humor” workshop and by becoming the instructor of the “Improv for Scientists” class he once took. His whole goal for 2023 was to pursue improv and comedy more, which he did by performing stand-up, storytelling, and improv around the community.

Ben continued as a postdoc working full time with John Garrett starting in January 2024 and shifting fully to more imaging and biomedical research. He continued to perform biostatistical research investigating how CT-derived tissue measures can predict survival and began researching large language models as readers of radiology reports to identify potentially missed health issues. He also started Amalgam Improv to continue building improv in the Madison community. In November 2024, Ben started working as an Informatics Data Scientist in the Department of Radiology.

From 2025 to the present, Ben has kept braiding those threads together. He expanded Amalgam Improv with more shows, workshops, jams, and learning resources, and put his data science skills to work for the improv community by building the world’s largest improv group map alongside an Improv Festival Map. The mapping work also echoed his research with Alexandria Wong on geospatial links among CT-derived biomarkers, socioeconomic context, and healthcare access in central Wisconsin, a project supported by a 2025–2026 Radiology Internal Research & Development pilot grant. His mix of research, teaching, and performance continued to show up publicly: he emceed Wisconsin Science Festival programs, led Sci-prov and applied-improv workshops for scientists, presented large-language-model radiology work at the Society for Imaging Informatics in Medicine, and was selected for the NIH Clinical Research Loan Repayment Program. In spring 2025, Ben also joined the independent improv team Big Honey. Its 2026 collaborations with Amalgam included Big Honey’s Little Improv Festival and Amalgam’s second anniversary in July 2026.
